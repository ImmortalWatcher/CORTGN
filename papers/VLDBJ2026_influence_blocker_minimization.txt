Abstract
Online social networks have emerged as prominent platforms for individuals to rapidly share ideas and perspectives. However,
their swift dissemination capabilities also make them powerful channels for the spread of misinformation. Such dissemination
results in substantial economic and societal harm, highlighting the need for effective suppression. To support this practical
demand, this paper investigates the inﬂuence minimization (IMIN) and blocker minimization (BM) problems. The IMIN
problem aims to ﬁnd a node set of size k (the given budget) to block, such that the reduction in inﬂuence spread of a given seed
set S is maximized. The BM problem is the dual of IMIN, aiming to ﬁnd a node set of minimum cardinality to block, such
that the threshold condition is satisﬁed, i.e., the reduction in inﬂuence spread of S is not less than the given threshold η. Both
problems are NP-hard and involve a non-submodular objective function. Existing IMIN solutions incur high computational
costs and offer no approximation guarantees on inﬂuence spread reduction. To ﬁll the gap, we build upon the sandwich
strategy to develop the ﬁrst efﬁcient algorithm with data-dependent approximation guarantees for IMIN. In particular, we
design non-trivial (1 −1/e −ϵ)-approximation algorithms to optimize the proposed submodular bounding functions. For the
BM problem, the existing solution is of limited effectiveness and lacks theoretical guarantees for both node set cardinality and
threshold condition satisfaction. In this paper, we design a heuristic and two bicriteria approximation algorithms to address
the BM problem. The heuristic is efﬁcient and satisﬁes the threshold condition, but fails to ensure near-optimal cardinality.
The two approximation algorithms both provide guarantees on cardinality: the ﬁrst attains a tighter approximation ratio at
the expense of efﬁciency, while the second yields a slightly weaker ratio with improved efﬁciency. Finally, comprehensive
experiments on 9 real-world datasets are conducted to validate the efﬁciency and effectiveness of the proposed techniques.

1 Introduction
With the rapid development of the Internet, various online
social networks (OSNs) have thrived, immensely satisfy-
ing and facilitating the need for individuals to share their
perspectives. Leveraging the established connections among
individuals, ideas spread across OSNs through word-of-
mouth effects [12]. However, immense user bases and rapid
sharing abilities also make OSNs effective channels for
spreading misinformation, which leads to signiﬁcant harm,
such as economic damages and societal unrest [3, 36, 59]. As
a result, limiting the spread of misinformation has emerged as
an important and urgent task. To this end, one effective strat-
egyistoremoveasetofcriticalnodes(referredtoasblockers)
from the network. A natural question under this strategy is,
within a limited budget, how to identify a set of blockers
that maximally suppresses misinformation. Wang et al. [52]
ﬁrst formulate this as an optimization problem, referred to as

inﬂuence minimization (IMIN). Formally, given a graph G,
a seed set S, and a budget k, the IMIN problem aims to ﬁnd
a set of k nodes to block such that the reduction in inﬂuence
spread of S is maximized. In addition, in real-world infor-
mation diffusion, user engagement may reach a tipping point
beyond which the diffusion process unfolds like wildﬁre [15,
35]. Hence, those seeking to suppress misinformation would
like to prevent a target number of users from being exposed,
thereby avoiding the tipping point and curbing its explosive
spread. To support this practical requirement, Pham et al. [41]
formulate the blocker minimization (BM) problem: given a
graph G, a seed set S, and a threshold η, the BM problem
aims to ﬁnd a blocker set with minimum cardinality such
that the threshold condition is satisﬁed, i.e., the reduction in
inﬂuence spread of S is not less than the given threshold η. In
this paper, we investigate both the IMIN and BM problems
under the IC model, and aim to develop algorithms that are
practically efﬁcient and theoretically grounded.
The IMIN problem is NP-hard and APX-hard under the
IC model [60]. To address the problem, Wang et al. [52]
ﬁrst utilize Monte-Carlo simulations to estimate the reduced
inﬂuence spread of seed set S given any blocker, and provide
a greedy algorithm to select blockers, i.e., iteratively select
the node that leads to the largest reduced inﬂuence spread.
However, their solution is prohibitively expensive on large
graphs due to the inefﬁciency of Monte-Carlo simulations.
Recently, based on the dominator tree (see Sect.2.4 for for-
mal deﬁnition), Xie et al. [60] design an efﬁcient approach
that can accurately estimate the reduced spread of S. On this
basis, a greedy method is ﬁrst proposed. Xie et al. further
observe that the greedy framework may miss selecting some
critical nodes. This motivates the development of a more
effective modiﬁed greedy algorithm, which prioritizes the
outgoing neighbors of seed nodes in S. However, the solu-
tions in [60] require re-estimating the reduced spread of S
after selecting a blocker in each iteration, which results in
prohibitive computation overhead, especially when the bud-
get k becomes large. Moreover, since the objective function
of IMIN is non-submodular, adopting the greedy method
cannot provide (1 −1/e)-approximate solutions [37]. Based
on the notions of curvature and submodularity ratio [4], we
further prove that applying the greedy method to the IMIN
objective cannot provide any meaningful performance guar-
antees on inﬂuence spread reduction.
To bridge the gap in IMIN, we propose SandIMIN, an
approximation algorithm which exhibits superior efﬁciency
and provides strong approximation guarantees. Building
on the sandwich strategy [34, 56], SandIMIN ﬁrst devel-
ops submodular lower and upper bounds for the objective
function, and then produces solutions with (1 −1/e −ϵ)-
approximations to maximize the lower and upper bounding
functions (breads of the sandwich). A heuristic solution for
IMIN is also produced (ﬁlling of the sandwich). SandIMIN
ﬁnally returns the best result among the three candidate solu-
tions.
The development of SandIMIN is non-trivial due to
the three challenges. Firstly, tight bounding functions with
the submodularity property (w.r.t. the IMIN objective) are
required to provide high-quality results. In particular, the
empirical effectiveness and approximation guarantees of the
sandwich-based approach rely on how close the proposed
bounds are to the objective function; a loose bounding func-
tion can be applied in our scenario, but it would fail to
produce satisfactory results. For example, a constant func-
tion can serve as a trivial submodular upper bound (e.g., an
upper bound that equals the number of nodes in the graph).
However, it is apparent that this bounding function may
adversely affect our results since the solution to the con-
stant function can be arbitrary. We address this challenge by
developing robust lower and upper bounding functions, with
their tightness empirically evidenced by SandIMIN’s superior
effectiveness and approximation ratio in our experiments.
Secondly, the proposed bounding functions are intractable
to compute in practice, and we prove that their computations
are both #P-hard. This necessitates efﬁcient and accurate esti-
mation methods. The widely used and powerful technique for
inﬂuence estimation, reverse inﬂuence sampling (RIS) [5],
cannot be trivially adapted to our setting. This is because,
in contrast with RIS that treats all the nodes equally and
samples the node uniformly, we only need to focus on the
nodes that are prone to be activated by the seed set S, since
only those nodes can contribute to the bounding functions.
That is, if we estimate the bounding functions using a simi-
lar manner of RIS, we need to sample the node based on its
probability of being activated by S. However, this probability
is #P-hard to compute [7]. To overcome this issue, we ﬁrst
deﬁne two new sample sets named CP sequence and LRR
set, and design efﬁcient approaches for their generation. On
this basis, the unbiased estimators for the bounding functions
are then established.
Thirdly, it is essential to derive the appropriate sample
complexity for bounding function maximization to ensure
both performance guarantees and practical implementation.
The sampling schemes used in existing inﬂuence maxi-
mization solutions (e.g., OPIM-C [43]) cannot be applied to
address this challenge, as their unbiased estimator for inﬂu-
ence spread differs in form from ours. In view of this, we
ﬁrst establish novel martingale-based concentration bounds
tailored to our unbiased estimators. With these results, we
develop advanced stopping conditions to correctly miti-
gate the redundant sample generation. Building on these
foundations, we design App-LB and App-UB, (1 −1/e −ϵ)-
approximation algorithms for lower and upper bounding
functions maximization, respectively.
The BM problem is also proven to be NP-hard under the IC
model [41]. To tackle the problem, Pham et al. [41] propose

In this work, we propose three more effective algorithms
for the BM problem. Our ﬁrst method, Heur-BM, follows
the general workﬂow of the approach proposed in [41] but
improves it by incorporating an unbiased estimator of the
objective function. This design enables Heur-BM to theoret-
ically satisfy the threshold condition with high probability.
In addition, Heur-BM attains high efﬁciency through a novel
two-stage algorithmic optimization. Nevertheless, Heur-BM
lacks any approximation guarantees in terms of the blocker
set cardinality, due to the non-submodularity of the opti-

bicriteria approximation algorithm App-BM. At a high level,
rather than optimizing the objective function as in Heur-BM,
App-BM targets the optimization of the submodular upper
bounding function, by generating a ﬁxed number of sam-
ples. We prove that when the input error parameter μ of
App-BM falls below a boundary value, the algorithm can
guarantee strong approximation on the blocker set cardinality
with only a minor violation of the threshold condition. How-
ever, we note that this boundary value is generally very small
in practice, which causes App-BM to require an excessively
large sample size and makes it mainly of theoretical interest.
This limitation motivates us to further design a more efﬁ-
cient bicriteria approximation algorithm App-BM+, which
also focuses on optimizing the upper bounding function,
but improves upon App-BM by employing a more reﬁned
sampling scheme. Speciﬁcally, App-BM+ adopts a trial-and-
error strategytodynamicallydecreaseμuntilthepre-deﬁned
stopping conditions are satisﬁed. This adaptive mechanism
enables App-BM+ to achieve substantially higher empirical
efﬁciency than App-BM. As the price of enhanced efﬁciency,
weprovethat App-BM+providesslightlyweakerapproxima-
tion ratios than App-BM. It is noteworthy that the underlying
idea of the two approaches resembles the sandwich strategy;
however, the key difference lies in the objective: our goal is
to minimize the blocker set cardinality, while the sandwich
strategy is devised for maximization problems. This intrin-
sically renders its theoretical results inapplicable to the BM
setting, thus necessitating new insights and making our anal-
ysis non-trivial.
Finally, extensive experiments over 9 real-world datasets
are conducted to verify the efﬁciency and effectiveness of
our proposed techniques. The experimental results demon-
strate that, for the IMIN problem, our SandIMIN algorithm
achieves orders-of-magnitude speedups while maintaining
competitive solution quality compared with the state-of-the-
arts [60]. Moreover, for the BM problem, our algorithms are
scalable and consistently deliver superior effectiveness over
the existing solution [41]. Note that this paper is a signiﬁcant
extension of our previous work [51]. The main contributions
of the paper are summarized as follows.
• For the IMIN problem, we design (1 −1/e −ϵ)-
approximation algorithms to maximize the proposed
submodular bounding functions. Building on these foun-
dations, we propose SandIMIN, the ﬁrst efﬁcient approx-
imation algorithm that provides strong data-dependent
approximation guarantees.
• For the BM problem, we ﬁrst propose Heur-BM, a
heuristic that guarantees the threshold condition satis-
faction. We then propose two bicriteria approximation
algorithms, App-BM and App-BM+: App-BM achieves
a tighter approximation ratio but sacriﬁces efﬁciency,
while App-BM+ attains improved efﬁciency at the
expense of slightly weaker ratios.
• We conduct extensive experiments on 9 real-world
datasets to evaluate the performance of our algorithms
on the IMIN and BM problems. The results demonstrate
substantial improvements over the corresponding com-
petitors across both tasks.

2.1 Diffusion model
We focus on the widely-studied independent cascade (IC)
model [5, 24, 43, 54, 55, 58]. Given a seed set S ⊆V , the
diffusion process of S under the IC model unfolds in discrete
timestamps.Attimestamp0,onlythenodesin S areactivated.
Each activated node will remain active in the subsequent
timestamps. If a node u is activated at timestamp t, then
for each of its inactive outgoing neighbor v, u has a single
chance to activate v with probability p(u, v) at timestamp
t + 1. The propagation process stops when no more nodes
can be activated.
2.2 Problem definition
Given a seed set S, let IG(S) be the number of active nodes in
G when the stochastic propagation process stops. We deﬁne
E[IG(S)] as the inﬂuence spread of S, where the expectation
is taken over the randomness of propagation. Alternatively,
the diffusion process can be characterized by live edge pro-
cedure [24]. For any given G = (V , E), by removing each
⟨u, v⟩∈E with 1 −p(u, v) probability, the remaining sub-
graph is referred to as a random realization (denoted by φ)
of G. Let Iφ(S) be the number of nodes reachable from S
in φ. The inﬂuence spread E[IG(S)] of S can be calculated
as E[IG(S)] = 
φ∈ Iφ(S) · p(φ), where  is the set of
all possible realizations of G, and p(φ) is the probability for
realization φ to occur.
In this work, we consider the node blocking strategy to
minimize the inﬂuence spread of a given seed set. When a
node u is blocked, we set the probability of all edges pointing
to u as 0 and refer to u as a blocker; nodes whose status
transitions from active to inactive due to this operation are
said to be protected by u. We assume that seed nodes cannot
be blocked. Given a seed set S ⊆V and a blocker set B ⊆
V \S, we denote DS(B) = E[IG(S)] −E[IG[V \B](S)] as the
expected number of nodes protected by B, i.e., the reduced
inﬂuence spread of S after blocking B, where G[V \B] is the
subgraph of G induced by V \B.

2.3 Relationship between IMIN and BM
The IMIN and BM problems are deﬁned over the same set
function D(·) but swap the roles of objective and constraint:
IMIN maximizes D(·) under a cardinality budget, whereas
BM minimizes the cardinality subject to a threshold on D(·).
In the following, we ﬁrst show that, under exact optimiza-
tion, the two problems are mutually reducible, i.e., an exact
oracle for either problem solves the other (Lemma 1). This
reduction, however, is of purely theoretical interest, since
both problems are NP-hard and thus intractable to solve
exactly.Wethenshowthatthereducibilitybreaksdownunder
approximation: an approximation guarantee for one problem
does not transfer to the other (Lemma 2). Consequently, each
problem calls for dedicated approximation algorithms.

approximation ratio is thus at most (k −1)/(k + M), which
vanishes as M grows. Hence, a β-approximate BM algorithm
yields an IMIN approximation ratio arbitrarily close to 0. ⊓⊔
2.4 Existing solutions revisited
2.4.1 Existing IMIN solutions
Given the hardness of the IMIN problem, existing IMIN solu-
tions [52, 60] are all based on the greedy strategy. In the
following, we ﬁrst outline the greedy framework for IMIN,
and then detail the state-of-the-art algorithms, along with
their empirical and theoretical limitations.
Greedy framework Let D(u|B) = D(B ∪{u}) −D(B) be
the marginal gain of adding u to B w.r.t. D(·). In a nutshell,
thegreedyframeworkstartsfromanemptyblockerset B.The
subsequent part of the algorithm consists of k iterations. At
each iteration, it iteratively selects the node v from V \S that
leads to the largest D(v|B) and adds it into B. After selecting
one blocker, the probability of all edges pointing to it is set as
0. Due to D(B) = E[IG(S)] −E[IG[V \B](S)], one feasible
way for calculating D(B) is to compute E[IG(S)]. How-
ever, the computation of E[IG(S)] is proved as #P-hard [7],
whichmeansthatthecomputationof D(B)isalso#P-hard.In
[52], Wang et al. use Monte-Carlo simulations to estimate the
inﬂuence spread E[IG(S)] and adopt the greedy framework.
It can solve IMIN effectively but due to the inefﬁciency of
Monte-Carlo simulations, it incurs signiﬁcant computation
overhead.
State-of-the-arts In comparison with the Monte-Carlo based
estimation method under the greedy framework, the state-
of-the-art solutions [60] optimize the estimation for D(·).
Instead of estimating the inﬂuence spread of S, Xie et al.
[60] explicitly estimate the reduced inﬂuence spread based
on dominator tree (DT) [2, 33], with the deﬁnition shown
below.
Deﬁnition 1 (Dominator) Given a realization φ and a source
s, a node u is called a dominator of a node v if and only if all
paths from s to v pass through u.
Deﬁnition 2 (Immediate Dominator) Given a realization φ
and a source s, a node u is said to be an immediate domina-
tor of a node v, denoted as idom(v) = u, if and only if u
dominates v and every other dominator of v dominates u.
Deﬁnition 3 (Dominator Tree) Given a realization φ and a
source s, the dominator tree of φ is induced by the edge set
{⟨idom(u), u⟩: u ∈V \{s}} with root s.
As presented, each DT has only a single root. Xie et al.
[60] ﬁrst propose to replace the seed set S with a uniﬁed
node s that exhibits an equivalent non-seed inﬂuence spread
under the IC model, i.e., E[IG(S)] −|S| = E[IG(s)] −1. In
particular, for each node u ∈V \S, if there are h distinct seed
nodes pointing to node u, and each edge has a probability pi
(1 ≤i ≤h), they remove all edges from the seed nodes
to node u, and add an edge from node s to node u with the
probability 1 −h

that for each u ∈V \S, the expected number of protected
nodes by u (i.e., D(u)) equals the expected size of the subtree
rooted at u in DT. With these results, the estimation procedure
in [60] runs as follows.
• Generate a number of random realizations G from G.
• For each realization φ ∈G, apply Lengauer-Tarjan algo-

3.3.3 A lightweight heuristic for IMIN
Thissectionfurtherproposesalightweightandsimpleheuris-
tic, Heur-IMIN, for the IMIN problem. Heur-IMIN selects the
blocker in a greedy manner from ON (the outgoing neigh-
bors of S), guided by the quality function s(·) deﬁned below,
where deg[v] is the out-degree of v.
s(v) = Pr[s →v] · deg[v].
In other words, the k nodes with the largest s(·) values are
selected as the blockers. Heur-IMIN is motivated by the fol-
lowing observations: i) the outgoing neighbors of the seeds
are more likely to be blockers; ii) the nodes with a relatively
high probability of being activated by s are more likely to
be blockers; iii) the nodes with large inﬂuence are more
likely to be blockers. If the budget is no less than the num-
ber of nodes in ON, we directly return ON as the blocker
set. As indicated by Theorem 5, Heur-IMIN does not make
any theoretical contribution to our ﬁnal algorithm SandIMIN.
Nevertheless, it may improve the empirical performance of
SandIMIN with almost no extra runtime cost.
4 Solutions for blocker minimization
This section focuses on addressing the BM problem, for
which we develop three algorithms. We ﬁrst present a
heuristic that ensures the threshold condition is satisﬁed
(Sect.4.1). We then design two approximation algorithms
with non-trivial theoretical analysis, striking a trade-off: one
achieves a tight approximation ratio at the expense of efﬁ-
ciency (Sect.4.2), while the other provides a slightly looser
guarantee with improved efﬁciency (Sect.4.3). Notably, the
approximation ratio here is deﬁned in terms of the blocker
set cardinality.

To address this limitation, this section designs App-BM,
a bicriteria approximation algorithm for the BM problem.
The main idea of App-BM is to optimize the submodu-
lar upper bound DU(·) using the Thre-Greedy framework
(Algorithm 1). Note that while this idea appears similar to
that of the sandwich strategy, a key difference is that our goal
here is to minimize the cardinality of the selected blocker
set, whereas the sandwich strategy is designed for maximiza-
tion problems. This intrinsically renders its theoretical results
inapplicable.
As shown in Algorithm 10, App-BM ﬁrst generates a cer-
tain number of LRR sets to compute the unbiased estimator
ˆDU(·) of DU(·), according to Lemma 6. Then, Thre-Greedy
is invoked to maximize ˆDU(·) until ˆDU(B) ≥(1 + μ)η,
where μ is a input error parameter.

directly, since it relies only on the statistical properties of the
estimators.

5 Experiments
In this section, we conduct extensive experiments to evaluate
the performance of our proposed algorithms on both IMIN
and BM problems. All the programs are implemented in C++
and performed on a PC with an Intel Xeon 2.10GHz CPU and
512GB memory. The source code is available at [1].
5.1 Experimental setup
Algorithms We evaluate the following algorithms on the
IMIN problem. (1) AG and GR: the state-of-the-art algorithms
proposed in [60]. (2) SandIMIN: the approximation algorithm

a simpliﬁed version of SandIMIN in which the App-UB sub-
routine is not executed for improved efﬁciency.
For the BM problem, we evaluate the existing solution (1)
Base [41], and our proposed three algorithms: (2) Heur-BM;
(3) App-BM; (4) App-BM+.

threshold satisfactory ratio offered by App-BM+ falls below
1 in a few datasets, indicating that App-BM+ may violate the
threshold condition in certain scenarios. This outcome is not
unexpected, as App-BM+ provides bicriteria approximation
guarantees, which permit minor violations of the thresh-
old condition. Furthermore, App-BM+ consistently requires
fewer blockers than Heur-BM, owing to its O(ln n) cardi-
nality approximation guarantee. Notably, although Heur-BM
provably admits no constant-factor bound on its cardinal-
ity (Lemma 11), in practice it does not fail drastically: over
all datasets and settings, it returns less than one order of
magnitude more blockers than App-BM+. To explain, the
unbounded worst case in Lemma 11 arises only when a few
nodes jointly protect a large region while each contributes a
negligible marginal gain, so that the greedy overlooks them;
such conﬁgurations are quite rare in practice.
Remark Theoretically, App-BM+ is favored over Heur-BM
for its strong approximation guarantee, which certiﬁes its
solution quality in advance. Empirically, our experimental
results show that on the small and medium datasets, App-
BM+ generallydominates Heur-BM, returningfewer blockers
while being faster than Heur-BM. On some large datasets,
however, a trade-off emerges: App-BM+ still returns fewer
blockers, but Heur-BM can run faster. In such cases, App-
BM+ is preferable when a small blocking set is desired,
whereas Heur-BM may be more suitable when runtime is
the dominant concern.
6 Related work
The inﬂuence maximization (IM) problem, which aims to
ﬁnd a set of users with the largest inﬂuence spread, is a fun-
damental problem in graph analysis. Kempe et al. [24] ﬁrst
formulate IM problem and propose independent cascade (IC)
as well as linear threshold (LT) models. In addition, they uti-
lize a greedy algorithm that returns (1−1/e−ϵ)-approximate
solution. Afterwards, a large number of work [7–11, 17, 23,
39, 44, 45, 55] focuses on the development of heuristic algo-
rithms to reduce the computation overhead. However, such
solutions return the results without any approximation guar-
antees. As a milestone, Borgs et al. [5] propose the reverse
inﬂuence sampling (RIS) technique for inﬂuence spread esti-
mation. Subsequently, many RIS-based algorithms [18, 22,
38, 43, 46, 47] that ensure (1 −1/e −ϵ)-approximations
with reduced computation overhead are proposed. To bet-
ter capture real-world scenarios, numerous variants of IM
have also been extensively investigated. For instance, [26,
31] incorporate temporal aspects, while [30, 53, 54] inte-
grate location information into the IM setting. Wang et al.
[50] study the IM problem with priority constraints. More-
over, the seed minimization (SM) problem, a classical dual
of IM, has been widely studied in the literature [14, 16, 20,
21, 28, 32, 42, 62, 64]. The goal of SM is to identify a seed
set of minimum cardinality that ensures at least η nodes are
inﬂuenced in expectation, where η is a predeﬁned threshold.
As the counterpart of IM, the inﬂuence minimization
(IMIN) problem has also attracted great attention due to its
wide applications (e.g., misinformation suppression) [3, 36].
Wang et al. [52] ﬁrst formulate and study IMIN under the IC

simulations. More recently, Xie et al. [60] design a novel
dominator tree–based approach to efﬁciently estimate the
reduction in inﬂuence spread, on top of which two greedy
methods are developed. However, we note that all exist-

any approximation guarantees. This paper ﬁlls this gap by

algorithm for IMIN. Beyond the node-blocking strategy, sev-

misinformation propagation. Under this strategy, the objec-
tive is shown to be monotonic and submodular. Leveraging
these properties, Tong et al. [48, 49] later design the sam-
pling based methods and present the algorithms that provide
(1 −1/e −ϵ)-approximations. In [27] and [25], the edge-
blocking strategy is investigated under the LT and IC model,
respectively.
Furthermore, as a natural variant of IMIN, the BM prob-
lem has been formulated and investigated in the literature. In
[40] and [41], Pham et al. study the BM problem under the
LT and IC model, respectively. In both works, the authors
propose scalable greedy algorithms equipped with tree-
basedestimationmethods.However,theirproposedsolutions
neither guarantee theoretical satisfaction of the threshold
condition nor ensure that the cardinality (of the selected
blocker set) is close to the optimal value. In this work, we
alleviate these limitations by developing practical algorithms
for BM with non-trivial approximation guarantees.
7 Conclusion
In this paper, we study the inﬂuence minimization and
blocker minimization problems under the IC model. For the
former, we propose the ﬁrst approximation algorithm with
superior efﬁciency and strong performance guarantees; for
the latter, we design a scalable heuristic and two bicrite-
ria approximation algorithms. Extensive experiments over 9
real-world datasets are conducted to verify the performance
of our algorithms. A promising direction for future work is
to study both problems under other diffusion models, such
as time-aware models.
